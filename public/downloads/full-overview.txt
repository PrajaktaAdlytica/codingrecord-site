CODINGRECORD — FULL OVERVIEW / NO JAVASCRIPT REQUIRED

Audit the evidence behind a claim before a human coder releases it.
A read-only audit concept. The coder keeps the decision.

CONCEPT PREVIEW / DEMO — SYNTHETIC DATA

01 Pre-bill anomaly sampling/prioritization
Bring potential documentation and code mismatches into a bounded review queue. Test whether prioritization improves the use of coder attention against the current audit baseline.
Input: Synthetic claim, existing proposed code and documented sampling scope.
Output: A prioritized exception with source, owner and a reason to review—not a coding determination.
Boundary: Sampling can miss exceptions. Evaluate a sample of unflagged records as well as flagged records.

02 Code-to-note evidence map for human coders
Put the proposed code beside its source excerpt, dated rule context and assigned reviewer. Make incomplete or conflicting documentation visible before a human disposition.
Input: One specialty or claim type, a synthetic note and fictional code placeholders.
Output: A read-only evidence map, an explicit uncertainty state and a human review trail.
Boundary: A link is provenance, not proof. Missing context opens review or abstention; it never creates a code correction.

03 NCCI/MUE version-aware edit checker
Compare an encounter against a specified source version and effective date. Keep the historical rule context separate from a comparison using current edits.
Input: Licensed code/rule sources, setting, service date and source/version scope—requirements for a future pilot.
Output: A source-linked edit exception with version, effective date and reviewer.
Boundary: Public source coverage is incomplete. Payer variation and missing historical context can require abstention.

04 Denial root-cause clustering and appeals evidence drafts
Group similar denial-review themes and assemble source-linked evidence drafts for a qualified reviewer. Test this separately from the initial evidence-map pilot.
Input: An authorized retrospective denial cohort and permitted supporting documentation.
Output: Illustrative clusters and unsent draft evidence for human review.
Boundary: No appeal is sent, no payer is contacted and no reimbursement outcome is promised.

05 Coder QA/training trend reports
Summarize review dispositions and recurring documentation questions, with their denominators and source versions intact.
Input: Attributed, human-reviewed exceptions and agreed trend categories.
Output: A training discussion with traceable examples—not an employee performance determination.
Boundary: Synthetic counts demonstrate the interface only. They are not evidence of quality, adoption or efficacy.

QUESTIONS

Is CodingRecord an operating product?
No. This is a research-stage concept and a working website preview. The product screens use synthetic records only. Demand, efficacy, adoption and production readiness have not been established.

Does it code or submit claims automatically?
No. The proposed starting point is a read-only evidence-to-code map for one specialty and pre-bill edit exceptions. The coder retains final code and submission authority.

What happens when evidence is missing?
The record remains unresolved or is marked abstained by the human reviewer. A missing source is not replaced by an assumed rule. There is no automatic correction or release.

Which rules and codes are in the demo?
Only clearly fictional placeholders such as CODE-A and DEMO-RULE vA/vB. No real CPT or ICD code content is included. The replay illustrates an interface, not validated coding behavior.

Can I connect an EHR or upload records?
No. There are no live integrations, uploads or external writes. Integrations are discovery questions for a future governed pilot. Do not enter patient information or clinical details.

What is the pricing?
Pricing is a hypothetical test, not an active offer. The proposed model is per specialty/site plus scoped setup, with a volume-based alternative to explore. Scope, rights, owner, term and review gates must be agreed before a paid shadow audit.

What would make a pilot ready?
Eight coding/compliance interviews, confirmed licensed sources, a blinded audit agreement, one paid shadow-audit sponsor and signed data-access/security and human oversight approvals. Precision, review-cost and coder-acceptance thresholds must be agreed with the sponsor.

What happens after I prepare an inquiry?
This preview creates a downloadable draft on your device. It does not send an inquiry or book a meeting. A future live flow would invite the relevant operations role to a one-specialty shadow-audit design session.

CODINGRECORD — CONCEPT 25 VALIDATION HANDOFF
Research-stage Medical Coding Auditor. CodingRecord is a provisional name.

THESIS AND CAVEATS
An evidence-to-code map may help a human coder inspect pre-bill exceptions. No demand,
revenue, adoption, efficacy, accepted price, live integration or operating service is
established. Rules are sources, not evidence of vendor revenue uplift. This is not
an autonomous coding system. Coder retains final code and submission authority.

BUYER AND FIRST CHANNEL
Revenue-integrity director or coding compliance lead at a health system or RCM provider.
Coders are users. Initial interviews through HIM/revenue-integrity associations and RCM
consultants; offer a one-specialty shadow-audit design session. No partnership implied.

FIVE SOURCE OFFERS (PRESERVE ORDER)
1. Pre-bill anomaly sampling/prioritization
2. Code-to-note evidence map for human coders
3. NCCI/MUE version-aware edit checker
4. Denial root-cause clustering and appeals evidence drafts
5. Coder QA/training trend reports
Select one initial module. The starting wedge is the read-only evidence map.

THREE FEATURED SYNTHETIC CASES
DEMO-CR-001: clean handoff; Reviewed; Coder A; source present in fixture.
DEMO-CR-002: ambiguous exception; Pending; Coder B; conflicting/incomplete references.
DEMO-CR-003: blocked case; Abstained; QA Lead; source version unavailable.
These sit inside a ten-record synthetic queue with pending, reviewed, unresolved,
rejected and abstained states. All names are roles, identifiers are fictional and
placeholders are non-billable. Dates and source versions are explicit. A reviewed state
means a fictional human event, not coding validity, billability or compliance.

SCREENSHOT STORY

No portal branding, real patient/provider identifier or validated decision.

VALIDATION SEQUENCE
Interview buyer/operator/oversight separately. Record the incumbent and observed baseline.
Show synthetic evidence review and test willingness to pay at a clearly hypothetical model.
Obtain bounded rights and a blinded retrospective replay of historical claims against
human-audited outcomes using licensed edits. Agree endpoint, thresholds and a stop rule.
Freeze original outputs. Keep historical effective rules separate from current comparisons.
Inspect unflagged samples. Report denominators, missing context, selection bias and uncertainty.

METRIC AND GATES
Primary: coder-accepted true-positive exceptions per 100 reviewed claims versus current audit.
Guardrails: false-positive minutes and overcoding flags. Also measure adjudicated precision,
coder acceptance/rejection reasons, unresolved/abstained proportion and reviewer disagreement.
Do not invent numeric thresholds. Agree them with the sponsor and budget/oversight owners.
Readiness: 8 coding/compliance interviews, licensed source confirmation, blinded agreement,
1 paid shadow-audit sponsor, signed data-access/security and human oversight approvals.
Stop if source/code/documentation rights fail or false-positive review cost exceeds value.

COMMERCIAL AND EXPANSION HYPOTHESES
Per specialty/site plus setup; test volume alternative. Hypothetical, not active pricing.
Expand across sites, payer-specific denials or CDI only after prospective review supports
rules maintenance and value. Contract potential depends on claim mix, incumbent stack,
audit cost and repeatable onboarding. A revenue thesis is an unvalidated account-price
and retained-account hypothesis. No invented TAM or total-healthcare-spend proxy.

INTERVIEWS
Buyer: which queue matters; incumbent benefit; compliance versus net revenue; budget owner;
what would justify a paid shadow audit; response to a clearly hypothetical price model.
Coder: process without records; source/rule search; abstention/rejection; false-positive time;
what evidence changes the decision; what stays under human control.
Oversight: permissions; licenses; gold labels; payer/version limits; liability; stop authority;
retrospective and prospective approval requirements.
